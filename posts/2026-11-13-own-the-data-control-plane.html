Strategy brief · Platform engineering

Own the data. Own the control plane. Let the apps come and go.

AI agents are becoming the main users of our software. When that happens, value moves out of individual applications and into two places: the data they act on, and the layer that decides what any agent or person is allowed to do. We should build both on purpose.

Apps & agentsMany, fast-changing, replaceable. Buy, build or generate.
Control planeOne governed way to act: identity, policy, tools, approvals, audit.
Kingdom of dataOwned, cataloged, trusted. The asset that compounds.
01 · What is changing

The business logic is leaving the application

For twenty years we bought and built applications that bundled three things: a database, business rules, and screens for people to click. Agents break that bundle. They don't need screens, and they can apply rules across many systems at once.

“The business logic is all going to these agents… They are going to update multiple databases and all the logic will be in the AI tier.” Satya Nadella, Microsoft CEO, B2G podcast, December 2024

Agents are the new users

Agents can call a platform far more often than people ever did, and in parallel. Industry now calls this “agent experience”: can an agent find a capability, call it reliably and recover when it fails?

Software is getting cheap

Generating a screen, a workflow or an integration is far faster than it used to be. Code we write once is less of a moat than it used to be.

Risk moves to the action

When an agent can change a cluster, a firewall rule or a customer record, the question regulators ask is “who allowed that, and where is the record?” Each app answering that differently is not defensible.

02 · The model

Three layers, with very different lifespans

Apps and agents on top will churn. The two layers underneath should be stable, owned by us, and built to outlast any vendor or model.

03 · Why it matters here

Today vs. the world we should build

Today: app by appWith a data kingdom + control plane
Adding an agentNew integrations, new credentials, new review for every tool it touchesRegister once; it inherits identity, policy and audit
Answering “who did this?”Stitch logs from each systemOne evidence trail across people and agents
DataCopied into each app, defined differently in eachDefined once, owned by us, readable by any approved consumer
Replacing a vendor or modelMigration project; logic trapped inside the productSwap the top layer; data and rules stay put
NFRs & complianceRe-proven per applicationEnforced centrally and inherited
Shadow AITeams wire agents straight to systemsThe governed path is also the easiest path

The risk of not doing this is that agents connect directly to hundreds of systems, each in its own language. That is ungoverned, hard to audit and impossible to change safely.

04 · What we build

Two durable layers, concretely

Control plane

how anything acts
  • Identity for agents: every agent is a named principal with an owner, scope and expiry. No shared service accounts.
  • Policy as code: entitlements, change windows and blast-radius limits evaluated on every call.
  • Tool registry: one catalog of approved capabilities (MCP and APIs), versioned and tested, so agents call tools, not raw systems.
  • Approvals: risk-tiered human-in-loop; low-risk actions run, high-risk actions wait for a person.
  • Audit & evidence: every request, decision and result recorded in a form auditors can use.
  • Evaluation: agents and tools tested against real scenarios before they get more autonomy.

Kingdom of data

what everything knows
  • Single inventory: clusters, services, owners and dependencies across public and private cloud.
  • Change and config history: what changed, when, by whom (person or agent).
  • Operational signals: telemetry, incidents and vulnerabilities joined to the same inventory.
  • Shared meaning: “application”, “owner”, “environment” defined once and used everywhere.
  • Data products with owners: each dataset has an accountable team, quality checks and lineage.
  • Access through the control plane: the same policies govern reading data and taking action.

The proposal is to treat these two layers as the product, and every portal, agent or vendor tool as a consumer of them.

05 · How we get there

Phased, with value at each step

Phase 1

Foundations

  • Unified inventory for one estate
  • Tool registry with a first set of governed operations
  • Agent identity and central audit
Outcome: one agent workflow runs end to end on governed rails.
Phase 2

Policy & scale

  • Policy as code and risk-tiered approvals
  • Change history and telemetry joined to inventory
  • Onboard other teams' agents
Outcome: new agents onboard without a new security review per tool.
Phase 3

Default path

  • Control plane mandatory for automated change
  • Vendor tools consume our data, not the reverse
  • Evaluation gates for autonomy
Outcome: apps and models are swappable; data and rules stay ours.
06 · Fair challenges

What leadership will reasonably ask

Isn't this just another platform rewrite?

No. We keep existing systems of record. The control plane sits in front of them and the data layer reads from them. Phase 1 wraps a small number of high-value operations; it doesn't replace anything.

Won't vendors give us this out of the box?

Each vendor will offer a control plane for its own product. Ours has to span all of them, plus our private cloud, under our policies. That cross-vendor layer is the one thing no vendor is positioned to own for us.

Agents work fine with existing tools. Why build anything new?

Agents can use existing tools, and good tools for agents tend to be good for people too. The gap is governance: consistent identity, policy and evidence across every tool. That part doesn't come with any single tool.

What if the AI wave slows down?

Then we still have a clean inventory, one audit trail, and centrally enforced controls. Every part of this pays off for human operators and compliance even with no agents at all.

The ask

Fund the two layers that outlast the apps

  1. Name the control plane and the data foundation as strategic platforms with dedicated ownership.
  2. Approve Phase 1, scoped to one estate and one end-to-end agent workflow.
  3. Set a direction now: new automation and agents act through the control plane by default.
